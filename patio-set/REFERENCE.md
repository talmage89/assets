Chair/back/arm curves, slat pattern, corner connections, two-tone seam layout,
vent shape, ties and branding are authored designs. The closed canopy's folds
are an analytic gathered shell; it is a separate static variant, not a cloth
simulation. Gauges (2 mm tubular aluminum; 1.5 mm textile envelope; 6 mm hinge
quadrants), small fasteners and clearances are manufacturing estimates.

The original chaise uses clampable indexed hinge quadrants rather than the
reference's rear ladder brace. Both lock handwheels are separate, with X axes;
the backrest is adjusted after loosening them. No claim of certified load
capacity is made. Actual ground contact, assembly contacts, pole clearance,
mesh dimensions and sampled motion are measured by the construction checks.

## Accepted-set refinement

Installed reference photographs add evidence of use: Acabashi's garden umbrella
shows actual seam wrinkling, vent support and dirt specks; Pew Nguyen's cafe set
shows used outdoor furniture, scuffed legs and spotted tabletop surfaces. The
latter supplies the new photo-matched comparison. Its age, exact dimensions and
lens are unknown. Four table-face anchors estimate the camera using our original
standard-height table; no photo outline is used to generate geometry. The
umbrella is moved behind/right of the table for that comparison shot only,
matching the reference arrangement. Normal delivery retains the centered pole.

The open canopy now has 75 mm mid-panel hem drop and 35 mm inward hem scallop,
16 mm source rib-pocket tapes, stitched bound hem, and a 23 mm vent valance.
Those are authored construction estimates checked against the photographs.
Closed gores retain those bound edges, pocket seams, valance and gathered hem.
Default WEAR=0.35 now represents visibly weathered outdoor use: exposed cloth is
lighter than backs/undersides/crease valleys; powder-coat tops chalk; handling
and foot-height abrasion expose aluminum; water-ring deposits stay on upward
faces; pollen/dirt collect at sheltered intersections and ground splash zones.
