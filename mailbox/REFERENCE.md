# Measured basis

The USPS Historian's [size chronology](https://about.usps.com/who/profile/history/pdf/curbside-mailboxes-size-chronology.pdf)
records the familiar size-1 envelope: **19 × 6.5 × 8.5 in** (482.6 × 165.1 ×
215.9 mm). It also explains that T1 later became a capacity class, rather than
one mandatory envelope. This asset uses the historic shell proportions, not a
claim that all modern T1 products have identical dimensions.

[USPS installation](https://www.usps.com/manage/mailboxes.htm) gives 41–45 in to
the mail floor, a 6–8 in curb setback, and a nominal 4×4 wooden support. Chosen
floor: **42 in / 1066.8 mm**. The post is dressed 3.5 in / 88.9 mm square.
Ground is the road-height datum; terrain/curb setback belongs to scene placement.

[USPS-STD-7C](https://about.usps.com/publications/engineering-standards-specifications/spusps-std-7c.pdf)
requires an outward/downward door, at least 100 degrees opening, a right-hand
signal flag, and a raised metal floor. The authored door opens 105 degrees;
flag raises 90 degrees. The original rear panel, 0.8 mm sheet, returned door,
2 mm radial offset, end-pin hinge, catch, and timber half-lap dimensions are
construction choices, not measured manufacturer internals.

The [manufacturer photograph](https://www.architecturalmailboxes.com/products/elite-post-mount-mailbox/)
shows stamped galvanized steel, rear seam, front returns, flag and roof catch.
Its modern assembled envelope is 8.88 H × 6.92 W × 20.05 D in. Camera matching
therefore compares construction/proportion, not an exact reproduction. Eagle,
brand and approval markings are not copied. Address 1847 is fictional and drawn
with installed DejaVu Sans (open font). Only our scripts produce asset texels.
[Mounting instructions](https://www.architecturalmailboxes.com/wp-content/uploads/2022/12/E1100_E11_PostMount_Elite_NA_Document_InstallationInstructions.pdf)
show wood adapters and side screw fastening. See [refs/SOURCES.md](refs/SOURCES.md)
for retained reference files and rights.
