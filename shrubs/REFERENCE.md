The boxwood is maintained but several weeks past shearing. Small uneven lobes
are deliberately much shallower than the maple's scaffold masses. The front
faces −Y; +Y models a more shaded house-facing side with fewer low leaves and
localized old brown-green blades. Twenty-four slender basal stems branch low;
the foliage reaches to roughly 6 cm above grade. The former hard soil polygon
has been removed; the rooted stem ends meet the receiving scene's ground.

Jim Robbins' Green Velvet near porch columns is the primary hue/material and
regrowth reference. Niemiera's clipped rounded specimen is the second form
reference; its cultivar is not treated as Green Velvet. His house-foundation
photo establishes the much darker shrub values relative to a lawn. These are
ordinary planted settings with summer/leaf-on appearance, not controlled
color charts. Different specimens, unknown lens, pruning history and lighting
limit quantitative matching. Pixel fits are comparisons, not surveys.

Ilouque's mophead beside a garage is the primary hydrangea form/shadow reference.
Jim Janke's ordinary house bed is the second pair: the crop contains overlapping
plants, so its mass and camera fit are approximate. OShen's close-up supplies
serration, venation and sepal-form evidence. The source uses outward-arching
stems, opposite blades, interior branchlet cards and thirty-six nodding/upright
heads. The two outdoor photos have very different exposure/shadow populations;
report both rather than treating one median as a calibrated material value.
