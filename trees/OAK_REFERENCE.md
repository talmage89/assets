# Pin oak / research and authored specimen

A summer street pin oak (*Quercus palustris*), about 25 years old: target
16.7 m tall, about 10 m across, 45 cm nominal bole diameter. These dimensions
are authored within the brief, **not measured ages/dimensions of a photograph**.
Published mature species ranges are larger: Kentucky gives 18–21 m high and
7.6–12.2 m spread; this is a younger street specimen.

The architecture follows OSU, UConn and Kentucky: one central leader, many
slender lateral limbs, downward at the base, level in the middle, ascending
above. The living crown is limbed to approximately 2.5 m. Dead pins remain
on the lower bole (Yale and Ohio State); those do not imply lower living leaves.

The two primary summer comparison photos are OSU's open-grown tree and Stony
Brook's street/campus planting. Both have layered, interlocking crowns rather
than round masses. The OSU apex is partly clipped; Stony Brook's left side is
partly occluded by a neighbor. Camera anchors disclose those uncertainties.
The latter may be older/larger than the authored specimen; no survey supports
an exact size/age match. WWU's July photo is young and used only for surfaces.

Leaves will be alternate, 8–13 cm long, 5–7 deep bristle-tipped lobes with
rounded U sinuses, glossy dark upper faces and paler lower faces. These are
within OSU's 7.5–15 cm species range. **Checkpoint blades remain flat prototypes**:
no finished pinnate vein, cup, bristle, cuticle or wear work is claimed.

Young limbs are smooth gray-brown; the bole has shallow interrupted ridges,
with a subdued gray sheen rather than the maple's dark flaky plates. The
shared original analytic field is parameterized for lower relief and milder
fissure contrast. Its delivery bake is held until structure review.
