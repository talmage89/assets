# Builder colonial: reference and authored dimensions

This is an original 1990–2010 American builder-colonial grammar, not a copy of
one photographed house or purchased plan. References supply construction
vocabulary, dimensional bounds and proportion checks. Nothing is traced,
projected, imported as geometry or sampled into an asset texture.

## Evidence and limits

The [Maryland SHA 2019 suburbanization context addendum](https://www.roads.maryland.gov/OPPEN/Suburbanization%20Context%20Addendum_Final-2019.pdf),
Appendix F, identifies modern-colonial two-story massing with an attached
garage set forward and a shallow entrance porch. It supports the type, not
this design's dimensions or a claim about the photographed houses' dates.

The `colonial-1991-oh` reference, from northeast Ohio,
is listed as built in **1991**. Its photograph shows a two-story principal
block, six-over-six grilles, paired shutters, siding, side-gable roof and
front-facing double garage. Our first proof has a hip main roof and a room
over the garage; the comparison crop judges the principal facade. The photo
has no survey or reliable focal metadata.

The `colonial-rog-ri` reference, from Rhode Island,
shows the room-over-garage variation, front gable, corner posts, window trim,
and deeper sill/casing shadows. It also has two single doors, a projecting
entrance bay and a higher stepped foundation. Those are **not** features or
measurements of our proof house. The image's 2017 watermark does not establish construction date.

The 4 ft overlap is a **union**, not two complete houses placed through each
other. Intersecting wall skins are removed below the adjacent roof; windows
are rejected in covered wall areas; the garage owns the overlapping first
floor area and the lower room above. Rooms in the main block stop at that
interface. No hidden floor slabs, framing or interiors consume the mesh budget.

Roof planes are clipped at their actual intersections. Hip variants need a
narrow, tapered metal cricket where the garage would otherwise create a dead
valley. That saddle is geometry with falling side valleys. Gable variants
have diagonal draining valleys and need no saddle.

## Seed grammar and limits

Default ranges: principal width 38/40/42 ft, depth 26/28/29 ft; garage width
and depth 22/23/24 ft; projection 4/5/6 ft; main roof hip or gable at
6/7/8:12; garage left/right; upper-front rhythm four/five bays; gabled/hipped
portico; front veneer on/off; six restrained color palettes. Windows obey
room roles rather than independent random positions. Four lower-front bays
reserve entry space; the garage connection removes occluded ones.

`generate(seed, overrides)` accepts the same small nested plan as the JSON
CLI input. Two rectangular two-story blocks and a portico are the supported
grammar. This run deliberately keeps the room-over-garage variant. One-story
garages, arbitrary polygons, dormers and other house types are future grammar
extensions, not unvalidated random fallbacks.

Reference downloads, authors and rights: [refs/SOURCES.md](refs/SOURCES.md).

## Postwar ranch: research and authored grammar

This is a second, original exterior-shell type. The NPS Arkansas 1945–1970
context supports regional ranch adaptations, picture glazing and iron porch
posts. The NPS ranch resource study supports a long single story with wide
eaves. The leaf-on California 1966 and Oklahoma references show low roof/wall
ratios and strong soffit shade; the Ohio August street adds a third region.
The Minnesota photograph is spring supplemental evidence, not a summer photo.
Exact years are not independently known for the Ohio/Minnesota houses. Sources,
rights and direct image links are in [refs/SOURCES.md](refs/SOURCES.md).

The 1955 USDA *Wood-Frame House Construction*, Handbook 73, recommends at
least 8 in foundation-wall separation from finish grade, at least 18 in to
the joist underside in crawl spaces, and prefers 8 ft clear ceilings. These
are period construction references, not a permit certification. Our slab
floor elevations of 8/12/16 in and 24 in crawl floor are authored modules;
the modeled brick can run lower than the protected wood framing. Low-slope
3:12 roofing assumes the underlayment specified by the shingle manufacturer;
that concealed membrane is documented rather than given hidden polygons.

### Ranch masonry and source-value revision

Modular coordination uses 8 in length and three courses per 8 in height,
consistent with BIA Technical Note 10 and the modular sizes cited above.
Our geometry has a 72 mm veneer-face offset, 97.5 mm measured sash recess,
sloping individual rowlock bricks, and 4 mm steel lintel angles. The shell's
hole sides expose the masonry returns rather than applying white casing over
brick. The returns, angles and sill slopes are separately audited on actual
mesh surfaces. Most clear roof projections beyond veneer are 24.17 or 27.17
in; the plain seed has 18 in clear. Nominal roof-datum projections therefore
include the veneer offset; they are not reported as the clear measurement.

The new sunny North Carolina comparison is a brick ranch from a district
whose [municipal preservation record](https://raleighnc.gov/planning/services/battery-heights-historic-district) and National Register context date its
main development to 1956–64. This is a period attribution, not a verified
individual house year. Its original October 2020 photograph has direct sun,
a visible end wall and strong shade from broad eaves. A separate Nebraska
Commons photo is catalogued as a house built in 1960, taken October 2013.
It is a near-front oblique and supplies unobstructed sun/shade brick and roof
patches. It supplements the three-quarter pair rather than pretending to be
one. Photographers, rights and only the direct image URLs appear in SOURCES.

The source measurement tool uses fixed photo patches and projected world-space
patches on unoccluded brick/roof. It reports encoded-sRGB Rec.709 Y′ and decoded
linear luminance separately, with the camera exposure stated. A processed
photograph does not independently identify albedo. Brick body targets are
linear (.235,.113,.068) red, (.165,.078,.050) red-brown, (.39,.28,.17) buff,
and (.29,.205,.135) tan before fired-unit variation. Neutral mortar is
(.33,.305,.257), recessed 9 mm in the original normal field. No photograph
is opened by the material generator. The material chart's stored values
allow normalized vertex tint to brighten localized salts without exceeding
one; physical base brick color remains the chart multiplied by its tint.

## Postwar Cape: form checkpoint

The Cape is an original bounded exterior grammar, with a small 1940–1955
principal box and later owner additions. The [NPS suburban history](https://www.nps.gov/subjects/nationalregister/upload/NRB46_Suburbs_part1_508.pdf)
(p. 66), [PHMC field guide](https://www.phmc.state.pa.us/portal/communities/pa-suburbs/field-guide/cape-cod.html)
and NCHRP Report 723 (p. 100, registered in SOURCES) support the half story,
steep side gable, centered three/five-bay facade, small dormers, wood sash,
clapboard/shingles and detached or covered-connected garage. The Illinois
preservation survey supplies Midwest examples dated about 1946 and 1946–47,
as well as earlier elongated Capes. Its leaf-on photographs have no verified
capture season. A Massachusetts renovation publication dates its original
house to the 1950s; the summer leaf-on photos show later alterations.
The New Jersey and New York Commons houses have unverified individual years.
The New York April view is supplemental spring evidence, never called summer.

The circa-1940 NARA drawing, NAID 5928284, explicitly labels **8 ft first-floor
and 7 ft 6 in upper ceilings** and wood siding. Its irregular long plan is
not reproduced. The 1955 USDA Handbook 73 supplies period frame, cornice,
flashing, sash and foundation construction vocabulary. Modern manufacturer
10:12/12:12 Cape terminology is an additional dimensional comparator, not
evidence of postwar specifications. No source establishes surveyed dimensions
for the photographed homes; the following dimensions are authored bounds
from the brief, tested against those photographic proportions.

the rear: their level transverse intersection is a **pitch break**, not a
level valley trough. Actual drainage falls across that line. All other
retained valleys have positive longitudinal fall. The low-slope shed assumes
an appropriate concealed underlayment assembly; this form gate does not model
or certify roofing-system engineering.

## Cape source look-development measurements

white/pale-gray paint control. These conditional display comparisons do not

## Cape delivered finish evidence

The straight-striated replacement product photograph establishes nominal
12 × 24 in dimensions and 11 in exposure. The used straight-striated photograph
shows staggered joints, face nails, butt shadows, and localized lower-wall
biological staining. Its clean upper paint face is the assumed neutral; the
clean face ROI measures paint response while the full pair retains the staining.
Two exposed nail heads per unit and a 4.37 mm butt projection are authored.
Depth, striation and unit layout are generated without tracing photographs.
The supplemental sill photograph demonstrates water-site paint loss, long
grain-following flakes and checked surrounding paint. Its teal paint is not
a hue target for the asset's off-white film. Credits and direct image URLs
are in `refs/SOURCES.md`; these inspection photographs are never committed.

Wood and vinyl shadow comparisons use equal course counts, established by
vertical-derivative autocorrelation in the fixed photo crops: 2.941 and 7.944
courses (aluminum: 6.211). The corresponding delivered physical crops contain
the same counts. The shadow-profile report folds scanlines at that period,
and reports the dark/lit ratio and midpoint-threshold width. Its illumination
is conditional, not a purported geometric measurement of a photographic lap.
Actual exported geometry is independently ray-measured: wood is about 14 mm
proud at the butt, updated vinyl/aluminum 10.5 mm, cement 4.37 mm.

Cedar's nine native photographic lower-face samples exclude upper overlap
shadows and keyways; export/source samples use independent material rays and
the authored unit boundaries, with the same lower-face rule. This measures
unit-to-unit value spread separately from the much darker overlap shadows.
The number of sampled shingles differs because the original photograph has
limited resolution; these are conditional distributions, not a population
survey of cedar products. The manufactured periodic field still repeats.

The six delivered seeds share canonical existing maps plus two rectangular
512 × 256 shingle color/normal pairs, cropped exactly from the filtered 4K
packed bake, and two separately baked 256² masonry color/normal pairs.
Splitting removes mixed-material deep mips and thousands of chart triangles
while retaining the same 1 MiB addition. Constant shingle ORM channels use their authored scalar parameters, eliminating the old packed bake's few mixed boundary texels, freeing ⅓ MiB for the Cape-only masonry fields at the original texel densities. Brick head/bed joints have millimeter-scale irregularity and 1.8 mm recess; concrete manufacturing adds bugholes and aggregate, with low height-driven mineral deposits. No existing family map changes. Macro weather is baked to RGBA8
vertex colors; high-frequency manufacture is carried by the texture fields.
Source/export material pairs use identical lights, cameras, paint controls
and exposure, with numerical bake loss reported separately from photo error.
The aluminum white-paint control is separately baked because chalk is additive;
multiplying the accepted blue seed cannot correctly recover white chalk. It
shares all eleven image URIs and does not change any of the six main palettes.
No curled roof tabs ship; the authorized alternative retains granule loss,
algae runoff and shaded moss without isolated block-like tab geometry.
