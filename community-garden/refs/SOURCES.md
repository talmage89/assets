Primary dimensional sources consulted as HTML (not downloaded):

- [Southern Forest Products Association, Standard Sizing](https://www.southernpine.com/resources/specifying-southern-pine-lumber/standard-sizing/): dry S4S 2×10 = 1½×9¼in; 2×12 = 1½×11¼in; 4×4 = 3½in square. Copyright retained; dimension table consulted only.
- [Angelus Block, Dimensions and Sizes](https://www.angelusblock.com/resources/cmu-basics/dimensions-and-sizes/): specified 8×8×16in unit is 7⅝×7⅝×15⅝in. Copyright retained; HTML dimension table only, no CAD/model or product imagery downloaded.
- [OSU Extension, Raised bed gardening, FS 270](https://extension.oregonstate.edu/catalog/fs-270-raised-bed-gardening): maximum 4 ft bed width for reach; framed and unframed construction. Current edition is a construction comparator, not evidence of a fictional installation date.
- [Iowa State Extension, Creating raised bed planters](https://yardandgarden.extension.iastate.edu/how-to/creating-raised-bed-planters): 3–4 ft two-sided reach and approximately 3 ft aisles for equipment.
- [2010 ADA Standards](https://www.ada.gov/law-and-regs/design-standards/2010-stds/): §§302, 304, 305, 306, 403 and 902. Firm/stable surface; 36in minimum route, 60in turn, 30×48in clear floor, 27in knee height, 28–34in work surface. Used to dimension the accessible assembly; this is not a certified site compliance assessment.
- [Seattle P-Patch new garden guide](https://www.seattle.gov/neighborhoods/p-patch-gardening/toolkit-for-gardeners/create-a-new-community-garden): accessible common facilities, rectilinear plots, neighborhood versus high-density site scales. Current guidance, period configurations corroborated by the 2014 report.
- [University of Minnesota crop spacing](https://extension.umn.edu/vegetable-growing-guides-farmers/crop-and-field-planning-tools-vegetable-farmers): tomato 18–24in, peppers 14–18in, kale 12–18in, corn 8–10in within rows, pole beans 3–4in. Our home-garden spacings are generally wider; not a claim of commercial maximum yield.
- [UMN summer squash](https://extension.umn.edu/garden-and-home/yard-and-garden/gardening-in-minnesota/growing-summer-squash-and-zucchini): basal crown, broad leaves, fruit below canopy and staged sowing.
- [UMN, Trellises and cages](https://extension.umn.edu/garden-and-home/yard-and-garden/gardening-in-minnesota/trellises-and-cages): conical commercial cages and deeply driven stakes; sprawling branches can exceed the cage. The garden uses a smaller determinate-type plant rather than claiming a full-sized indeterminate vine fits a small cone.
- [UMN, Growing beans](https://extension.umn.edu/garden-and-home/yard-and-garden/gardening-in-minnesota/growing-beans): twining pole beans about six feet or taller, six-foot stake/twine trellises and bamboo teepees installed at planting. Our 1.95m leader and 2.12m support are consistent with that class.
- [Trust for Public Land, Gardens of Their Own](https://www.tpl.org/resource/gardens-their-own-landpeople), 15 May 2010: apartment-gap gardens, durable fences, common water systems, gathering places and community ownership. Period land-trust context, not a dimension standard.
- [Seattle, Ballard P-Patch](https://frontporch.seattle.gov/2012/07/31/12th-annual-art-in-the-garden-at-ballard-p-patch/), 31 July 2012: enabled-gardener container beds and crushed-gravel paths funded together. Supports the 2012 accessible archetype's paired infrastructure.

Native library references for shed gauges, chain-link mesh and tube diameters,
ground profiles and context architecture remain in their own `refs/SOURCES.md`.
Botanical blade sizes are authored representative midsummer sizes, not surveyed
measurements extracted from unsurveyed garden photographs.
