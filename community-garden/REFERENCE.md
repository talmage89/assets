# Form decisions and source boundaries

The 1998 apartment-gap garden is informed by the Trust for Public Land's 2010
description of New York land-trust gardens: fenced gaps between apartment
buildings, raised beds, shared water and a gathering place. The 2004 garden
uses larger tenant plots at a park edge. DC's May 2012 Noyes Park concept
places its garden beside common walks, service access and picnic seating;
our plan uses the relationship, not its linework or a copy of its site.
The 2012 garden pairs container beds and compacted gravel paths, as Seattle's
July 2012 Ballard P-Patch announcement describes. The 2014 P-Patch report
corroborates standing-height beds and gardens on former parking lots.

OSU and Iowa State's guides cap two-sided reach at about four feet and allow
roughly three-foot working aisles. The garden's narrow framed beds obey that.
Ten-foot-wide tenant plots are field plots with spaced rows and internal walk
strips; they are not single ten-foot-wide framed beds that a gardener must
reach across. The larger plot's edge boards are lower, 19.05×139.7mm, and bare
plots have no perimeter lumber. Lumber beds use real butted ends and internal
posts rather than four intersecting infinitely thin walls. CMU walls have
two open cores per block and physical webs, not dark squares printed on top.

The ADA 2010 dimensions guide the accessible route and elevated tray. A tray
with 34in top and 27.5in underside gives shallow growing media and two-sided
18in reach. It does not masquerade as a waist-high soil box with no knee
space. Reach, slopes, firmness, turning and clear-floor dimensions are design
constraints; a fictional asset cannot certify a real site's compliance.
The renders retain the accessible court and route to the shared facilities.

Botanical scale choices are authored representative crop sizes, cross-checked
against garden photographs and Extension's spacing guidance. Before seeded
variation, tomato leaflets are 13.6–19cm on five leaders of a 1.72m plant;
peppers carry 17cm entire blades on a .76m forked plant. Zucchini blades are
36–46cm, beans 25cm on a 1.95m twining axis, and corn 78–92cm long by 10cm wide
on a 2.24m culm before its tassel. Lettuce blades span approximately 16–24cm,
kale 21–32cm and basil-like herbs 11cm. Stages scale both blade dimensions and
node population. These are neither species-level botanical diagnoses nor
surveyed measurements of individual photo plants.

Native shed geometry supplies skids, joists, stud framing, sheathing, roof,
returned trim, door braces and bored hinges. Only this garden's private copy
replaces individual fine shingle-tab cuts with full lapped gauged courses.
Their fine cuts belong in the later normal bake. Native fence gauges and
analytic alpha fields retain their own source citations. A small sag models
age without stretching the wire pitch; hand-tied trellises and hoses sag from
their supports and weight.

The earlier form-checkpoint photographs depict an installation day (Oliver, late
August; Commons/IPTC years disagree), a mature apartment-lot bed (WiseWords,
July 2010), and a neighborhood garden at street level (Martin Drive, July
2009). They anchor construction, growing scale and place respectively. Photo
fits retain their estimated lenses and residuals. White balancing is only a
local review operation; the references never become textures or mesh input.


## Midsummer growth revision

The look-dev revision keeps the accepted beds, paths, construction and care states.
Mature indeterminate tomatoes now reach 1.72m above soil (about 5ft 8in), with
five leaders, compound leaves, supplemental overlapping branchlet cards and
fruit trusses. This supersedes the earlier small determinate habit. The UMN
indeterminate photograph in `refs/tomato.jpg` is the direct growth comparator.
UMN's home-garden guide permits 2–3ft between vining plants; the 4x8 beds retain
.76m / 30in spacing, and the park uses two planted ribbons. Low companion herbs
occupy the light soil margins beneath the tomato canopy.

Zucchini blades are .36–.46m long and wide (14–18in), on radial petioles from a
short crown. Mature sweet corn is 2.24m / 7ft 4in before the tassel; leaves arch
and roll rather than forming flat horizontal rungs. Pole beans retain the
1.95m leader and actual bamboo/twine support radius, with overlapping alternate
trifoliate nodes. Marigolds form branching pinnate mounds with several flowering
and spent heads. These are representative authored plant dimensions, not lengths
surveyed from uncalibrated photographs.

The expanded photo register in `refs/SOURCES.md` identifies the botanical images
and a third period midsummer garden photograph (18 July 2010). Camera landmark
residuals measure framing assumptions only. The City Hall comparator's cage
scale is inferred from its visible hoop; its clipped bed ends cannot establish
precise bed dimensions. No image pixels are projected, traced or baked into an
asset texture.

The field guide is linked in the source register. Its commercial intervals are
context, not a claim that this mixed community garden targets maximum yield.
Teepees plant six vines around their measured pole feet. All existing fresh,
fallow and weedy care states stay; the revision fills the usable growing area
instead of enlarging the accepted beds. Botanical photo comparisons use assumed
neutral concrete (tomato) and black weed cloth (squash). Bean, corn and marigold
photographs have no reliable neutral and support morphology review only.

## Delivery corrections

The park's eight tenant plots are unframed cultivated openings in the turf.
Their hilled rows return to grade at the soil edge. The adjacent recreation
hall is an original representative building, 13 x 8.5 m with a 5.05 m ridge,
not a surveyed or traced building. Its siting follows the park-building
relationship in the municipal reference; its dimensions are authored.

Summer turf reuses the library's original Ground blade field and baked normal
and ORM maps at their actual 3.048 m repeat. A private warmer, lighter albedo,
vertex tints for mowing and dry/worn areas, crossed fine blade cards and rooted
clover change the garden without rewriting that delivery. Neutral assumptions
for the garden pairs are Wise's gray stucco, Martin's pale house siding and
City Hall's weathered gray board cap. Different photographic exposure and sun
remain limits on numerical color comparisons.

Corn ears now project upward from actual leaf joints, with a tapered husk,
short bracts and brown tip silk. The imported surface audit measures their
axis and length instead of quoting the generator's direction constant.
Mature lettuce and kale each have eight deterministic rosette variants;
placements also vary scale, angle, stage and color and retain failed gaps.
The generator's leafy rosettes represent mixed greens, not a taxonomic
replica of a particular cabbage cultivar.
