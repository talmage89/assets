# Dimensions and construction basis

This is the **form and look checkpoint**, set around 2005, for maintained original
American rental housing. It is neither a measured reproduction nor a building
permit model. References are registered in [refs/SOURCES.md](refs/SOURCES.md).
No downloaded art enters the meshes or material graphs.

The 1977 National Bureau of Standards *Three Proposed Typical House Designs
for Energy Conservation Research*, drawing 2-6 (PDF p.25), dimensions a typical
townhouse at **20 × 32 ft**, with living/dining at one end, a family room at
the other and stairs/services in the middle. The report describes typical
construction rather than a design to copy. The grammar uses that footprint
for the 1974 row, 20 × 34 ft for the 1985 row, 24 × 38 ft with a front garage
for 2002, and 18 × 32 ft for the compact 1979 row. Those departures are authored
choices within the brief's 16–24 ft width range.

The Sacramento Commons cultural-resources appendix describes the 1960 and
1962 garden apartments' stepped footprints, breezeways, wood frame, continuous
overhangs and private patios, with upper and lower outdoor spaces facing
different directions. DAHP's *Mid-Century Modern Architecture in Washington
State*, PDF p.113, provides four photographed mansard apartment examples dated
c.1970, 1972 and 1978. The 2004 used-building photograph provides a contemporaneous
brick/wood/shingle maintenance and proportion witness. It has **two stories**;
it is not evidence of a three-story plan. The generator's three-story type is
an original extension of the brief's walk-up typology.

HUD's MPS resource page lists Handbook 4910.1 (1994); the July 24, 1974 Federal
Register identifies the 1973 MPS Volume 2 for multifamily housing. These
establish the period regulatory context. They are **not** the source of the
specific stair dimensions below. Modern published Access Board dimensional
criteria provide a conservative comparator for maintained stairs and replacement
rails. Local historical egress exceptions and complete occupancy/code analysis
are outside this art checkpoint.

All apartment wings have four units per floor. The four principal buildings have
12 / 16 / 24 / 8 units: 1967 enclosed-core gable, 1978 L with breezeways,
1986 offset open cores, and 1973 straight breezeway/mansard. The two larger
forms have independently framed wings connected by a low supported open
canopy, not intersecting roof solids. These are linked-block variants, not a
claim of seamless arbitrary L-roof synthesis.

Internal unit rooms are shallow window backings at this stage. Shared stair
circulation is actual geometry; apartment floor areas are gross plan modules,
not independently habitable interior layouts. Do not infer full interiors,
fire ratings, accessibility certification or permitted occupant loads.

The look gate keeps the accepted 28 × 25 ft and 24 × 32 ft garden modules,
nine-foot stories and stair geometry. Its facade/core offsets are authored
2–4 ft construction choices, not a new dimensional claim about the photographs.
The additional seed 305 has four 38 × 25 ft gross unit reservations per floor,
two floors, a central enclosed stair, buff brick below a cedar mansard, and
5 ft deep recessed balcony wells. It is a type comparator for the used 2004
photograph; it does not claim that property's measured floor plan.

Townhouse front steps now alternate 3 and 4 ft, retaining the accepted six-inch
finished-floor changes. The garage bays project another 1 m, with their own
front gables, alternating brick/siding and arched stair fanlights. The new
wall-footprint polygons and walking sockets follow these physical steps.
