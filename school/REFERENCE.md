# School / measured references and original design choices

This is a **form and construction checkpoint**, before school look-dev. All
dimensions below are authored, checked against the cited comparators; none is
presented as a survey of a photographed school. Photographs never become maps.

## Planning evidence

Abigail Christman's 2016 National Register multiple-property submission,
[Colorado's Mid-Century Schools, 1945–1970](https://npgallery.nps.gov/GetAsset/9ec89ff6-366e-4efd-8045-dbc5683d86d0),
is the principal architectural-history source. Section E, pp 33–37 distinguishes
the single-story finger school, clustered classrooms around covered/glazed
connections, and the later internally organized open plan. Its illustrations
were inspected, rather than treating the text as a substitute for plans:

- PDF p72 / Figure 2: Orchard Avenue, 1948, with the classroom module, central
  circulation, multipurpose room, kitchen/boiler adjacency and separate kindergarten.
- PDF p83 / Figure 13: Fairview's original fingers and the later addition which
  closes their ends. A high-school massing comparator, not an elementary-room size.
- PDF p89 / Figure 19: Cheltenham's classroom school and 1970 teaching-area
  addition, with common circulation and clearly differentiated communal rooms.

The three scripts use these relationships in original plans. They do not trace
those drawings or reproduce any specific school. The compact seed represents
an internally organized 1970s school later partitioned into classrooms; its
central media/common area remains spatially distinct from the perimeter rooms.

[HABS LA-368 / Phillis Wheatley Elementary](https://tile.loc.gov/storage-services/master/pnp/habshaer/la/la0600/la0648/data/la0648data.pdf)
documents a very different elevated 1955 school. It informs the distinction
between structural skeleton, infill and glazing, not this family's ground-level
massing. The original districts' budgets, climates and programs differed.

## Construction and interpretation limits

Steel/aluminum ribbon sash is set into real openings, with gauged reveals,
lintels, precast sills, transom bars, mullions and selective replacement infill.
Recessed backings and some blinds are visual room witnesses; they are not
furnished classrooms. Interior partitions reserve the 30 × 32 ft clear cells.
The tall gym retains exposed wall above adjoining lower roofs. Flat roofs have
coping folds, joints, scuppers, downspouts and equipment curbs. Original 1960s
pods also have low-slope gables. Main doors have separate hinge frames,
vision openings, exterior pulls, interior panic bars, kick plates and closers.

The authored schools are small neighborhood institutions with eleven or twelve
general classrooms, not complete school architectural programs. Office, media,
kitchen, boiler and communal volumes are present. Toilet/storage/circulation
reservations and room openings are schematic; no occupancy approval, complete
egress network, structural calculations, mechanical sizing or interior model is
claimed. These limits remain visible at this gate instead of being hidden by
finished materials.
